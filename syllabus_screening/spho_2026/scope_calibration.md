## Rule fixed before content decisions

Imagine a strong student with the listed syllabus, ordinary prerequisite general
physics, university mathematics and normal experimental reasoning. Supply the
complete statement, data, diagrams and every given model. KEEP if this student
has essentially all the physics needed for most meaningful work. REJECT only
when unsupplied outside theory controls the majority or central dependency.
Use BORDERLINE for a genuinely uncertain scope boundary or ambiguous statement.
Uninspected content is **pending**, never a fabricated BORDERLINE decision.

The syllabus is not a closed keyword whitelist. Force balance, friction, center
of mass, hydrostatics, elementary ray optics, ordinary circuit laws, dimensional
reasoning, calculus, graph interpretation, fitting and measurement uncertainty
are conventional general-physics foundations. Mathematical difficulty does not
change eligibility. An exotic story does not change the underlying physics.

A supplied specialized relation is usable when its variables and application
are sufficiently explained; its theoretical derivation need not be known.
An equation alone does not license substantial unsupplied conceptual machinery.
Experiments, MCQs and written problems receive the same physics-scope test.

Classify whole top-level problems. Keep internal (a), (b), numbered sections and
experimental analysis tasks together. A full paper needs separate top-level
questions, including separate MCQs. Shared PDF paths do not merge decisions.
Page ranges and within-page question labels retain the intended boundaries.
Semantic majority considers conceptual weight and dependencies, not a fixed
fraction of subparts. Peripheral extensions do not veto a useful whole problem.

## Boundary examples

* KEEP: a provided drag/constitutive force law analyzed with force, energy and
  oscillations; an apparatus analyzed with calibration, uncertainty and fields.
* BORDERLINE: roughly balanced meaningful work between ordinary mechanics and
  substantial unsupplied continuum-fluid theory, with neither peripheral.
* REJECT: a problem whose central work requires unsupplied quantum perturbation
  theory or general relativity. Special-relativistic Lorentz transformations are
  outside the stated scope when required but not supplied; a given mass-energy
  relation used in nuclear reactions is within the explicit nuclear domain.
