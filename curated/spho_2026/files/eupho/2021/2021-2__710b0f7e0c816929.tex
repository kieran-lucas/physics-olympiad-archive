{\large\textbf{Thread around a cylinder}}

One end of a thread is tied into a loop of length $L$ ${>}2\pi R$, and a
cylinder of radius $R$ is put through the loop. The coefficient of friction
between the thread and the cylinder is $\mu$. The free end of the thread is
being pulled parallel to the axis of the cylinder (as shown by arrow in the
photo) while keeping the cylinder at rest. If the length of the loop is longer
than a critical value, $L > L_0$, the loop can slide along the cylinder without
changing its shape, otherwise the friction ``locks'' it into a place and
increasing the pulling force would eventually just break the thread. Find this
critical value $L_0$. The weight of the thread is to be neglected; the thread
will not twist when being pulled.
