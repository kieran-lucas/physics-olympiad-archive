\textbf{2.\ \textsc{Nitrogen explosion} (8 points)} --- \textit{Päivo Simson.}

A half-sphere of radius $r = 0.1\,\mathrm{m}$ is filled with liquid nitrogen at
the boiling point temperature of $T_1 = 77.4\,\mathrm{K}$
($-195.8\,{}^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$). The other half is then firmly sealed onto the
first, creating a sphere containing liquid nitrogen and nitrogen gas, each
occupying one-half of the volume. The sphere is immediately thrown into
$T_w = 20\,{}^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ temperature water, where it floats exactly as
shown in the figure below. After some time, it explodes.

\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=0.39\linewidth]{figures/NBPhO_2023_Q2_fig1.png}\end{center}

The sphere is made of PCTFE plastic of density
$\rho_p = 2130\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, maximum tensile strength
$\sigma = 3.4 \times 10^{7}\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-2}}$ (above this stress, the plastic
will break) and thermal conductivity
$k = 0.84\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$. For liquid nitrogen, under the
conditions considered here, the latent heat of vaporization is
$\lambda = 2.0 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}}$, specific heat
$c_v = 2000\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ and density
$\rho_n = 808\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$. Molar mass
$\mathrm{M}(\mathrm{N}_2) = 28\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$. The ideal gas constant
$R = 8.31\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}$. Temperature dependence of saturated
vapor pressure of nitrogen is shown below.

\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=0.42\linewidth]{figures/NBPhO_2023_Q2_fig2.png}\end{center}

\textbf{i)} \textit{(1.5 points)} What is the wall thickness $d$ of the sphere?

\textbf{ii)} \textit{(1.5 points)} What is the pressure $p_2$ inside the sphere
right before it explodes? The outside pressure is
$p_a = 1.0 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$.

\textbf{iii)} \textit{(1.5 points)}What is the temperature $T_2$ of liquid
nitrogen right before the explosion?

\textbf{iv)} \textit{(1.5 points)}Calculate the mass of nitrogen that
evaporates inside the sphere before the explosion.

\textbf{v)} \textit{(2 points)} Estimate the time it will take for the sphere to
explode. The heat capacity of the plastic and the heat flux through the upper
half of the sphere can be neglected.
